About

I’ve been making things for print since I was 13, and I’ve spent 32+ years in design and 12+ in web development, keeping live websites online.

How I Work

A little creativity. A lot of curiosity. Absolutely no duct tape.

Okay, occasionally metaphorical duct tape. But only until I can build a proper solution.

I believe great digital experiences happen when design and technology work together, not when one is forced to accommodate the other.

Here’s what that looks like in practice.

Design is more than decoration.

My background in graphic design taught me to think about hierarchy, storytelling, accessibility, and how people actually experience information.

A beautiful interface is wonderful. A beautiful interface that makes sense? Even better.

Accessible first. Because good accessibility is good everything.

Accessibility is a priority for me, not a cleanup task. I try to understand who actually uses a site, whether that’s a traveler on a phone, someone navigating by keyboard or screen reader, or a staff member updating a page at the end of a long day, and build around them.

It also pays off elsewhere. Clear structure, meaningful headings, real alt text, and fast pages help people, and they are the same things search engines and AI-driven search (whatever we’re calling Generative Engine Optimization this month) use to understand a site. Good accessibility is good SEO.

Every site I get my hands on gets set up for it. Sometimes an older site can be coaxed into accessibility. Sometimes it’s better to start fresh and do it right from the beginning. I’ll tell you which one I think it is.

Build it right. Then build it to last.

I love solving problems, but I’d rather not solve the same problem every Tuesday.

I build things that are easy to look after and can grow with the people using them.

Because today’s clever workaround shouldn’t become tomorrow’s emergency.

The server is part of the story.

I don’t stop at the front end.

Where a site lives matters as much as how it looks. Hosting, backups, security and speed decide whether visitors stay, so I look after those too.

If something breaks, I want to understand why. If something works, I want to know how to make it better.

And if something mysteriously fixes itself, I’m definitely going to investigate.

Curiosity is practically a job requirement.

I ask a lot of questions. I want to know why something works, not just that it does.

I don’t need to know everything.

I do need to know how to find the answer, weigh the options, and decide.

Preferably before touching the live site.

Knowledge is meant to be shared.

Documentation, training, and thoughtful handoffs are part of the job.

I want the people using the sites and systems I build to feel confident managing them.

A successful project shouldn’t require a developer standing nearby with a magic wand.

In practice

  • I back up first. Before any change that is hard to undo, I take a copy of the database and settings, and I write down where it is.
  • I write it all down. Every job gets a write-up: the goal, before and after numbers, what changed, what is still open, and what I got wrong.
  • I audit for accessibility, not just speed. Headings, alt text, contrast, keyboard paths, and focus get checked on every site I touch, and the findings go in the same write-up as everything else.
  • I fix things so they stay fixed. Small add-ons and settings instead of changing the theme itself, so an update doesn’t undo the work.

The Road So Far

From pixels to platforms. And everything in between.

I started in design at 13, pasting up the yearbook. By 18 I was a paid editor at the OakLeaves, the student paper at Manchester College (now Manchester University) in Indiana, because I’d already learned the software on my high school’s weekly paper (PageMaker, before it became InDesign). Right place, right time. Later I went back to school at a technical institute in Jacksonville, Florida, for web development and information technology, and graduated summa cum laude and valedictorian of a class of two.

Print taught me that typography matters, deadlines are real, and sometimes the smallest details make the biggest difference.

Eventually, my curiosity wandered beyond the page and into the code behind it.

One thing led to another.

Design became development. Development became infrastructure. Infrastructure became security, performance, accessibility, and an ever-expanding collection of interesting technical challenges.

Today I build, manage, and fix live websites.

I still care deeply about how things look.

I just happen to care equally about what happens when nobody’s looking.
