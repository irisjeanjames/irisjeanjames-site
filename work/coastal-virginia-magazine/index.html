Coastal Virginia Magazine

Twenty-five years of stories, a whole new publishing platform, and a media library with more than 12,000 images. This one was never going to be a quick weekend project.

Twenty-five years of stories. A whole new chapter.

The magazine launched in November 2000 as HR Monthly, became Hampton Roads Monthly and then Hampton Roads Magazine, and is now Coastal Virginia Magazine. It turned 25 in 2025, with an anniversary issue to match.

A magazine website has to do more than look good. It needs to publish on schedule, support advertising, help readers find stories, and give the editorial team a system they can actually work with. Preferably without calling a developer every time an issue goes live.

Moving the archives without losing the plot

I helped migrate the magazine’s archive, with articles going back to 2010, from TownNews to WordPress. That’s a lot of stories, images, and history to bring along for the ride. Later, I moved the site off its original prebuilt theme and onto a Hello Elementor child theme customized for Coastal Virginia, giving the publication a more flexible foundation.

The issue isn’t finished until it’s published

Print deadlines have a way of making themselves known. I built the templates and digital issue bookcases for each of the six yearly editions, then helped make sure those issues actually went live on schedule. The website had to work with the rhythm of a real magazine, not the other way around.

Advertising pays the bills. Details matter.

I manage advertising placements through AdRotate Pro and support the site’s SEO reporting, so the team can see how the publication performs in search. An ad in the wrong place isn’t just a design issue. It’s a business issue.

One publication, a family of brands

The magazine’s annual regional awards programs are run by a third party. I didn’t organize the voting, but the site hosts the contest results, and I added the winners to the business directory (opens in a new tab). It’s a searchable, mapped directory with Best Of listings from 2019 through 2026, alongside the Be Well, Bridal, Dining, Eastern Shore, Home & Garden, Real Estate, and School / Education guides.

Coastal Virginia Magazine connects with Wine Fest, Burger Battle, and Idea House. I brought the magazine’s branding into the header of each related site so they feel like part of the same family, without pretending they’re all the same project.

CoVa Business, rebuilt

CoVa Business is the magazine’s sister publication. In the last few months I rebuilt its website (opens in a new tab) from an old, tangled pre-built theme and a stack of outdated plugins onto a Hello Elementor child theme. In the October 2026 audit it scored 91 (A-) overall.

The 2026 audit: a little less waiting, a lot more accessibility

In October 2026, I audited a site with more than 12,000 images and an initial C+ grade. One particularly unhelpful discovery: page caching was turned off, so pages were being rebuilt for every visit, with about 4.9 seconds before loading began. I enabled caching and tackled structural accessibility issues, including page headings, main-content landmarks, descriptive article links, and teal text that needed stronger contrast.

Then came the image library. More than 12,000 images needed alt text, which is not a job anyone does by hand. I used AI to draft the descriptions, reviewed what it produced, and wrote 12,035 of them into the database. To keep the gap from reopening, I added a rule that builds alt text from clearly named new uploads, so the library stays described as it grows. The software handled the repetition. I handled the judgment calls.
