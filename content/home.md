## Case studies
#### Every project has a story. Usually a plot twist.

I've worked with publishers, tourism organizations, entertainment brands, and businesses of all shapes and sizes.

Some projects started with a blank canvas. Others started with a website held together by outdated plugins, questionable decisions, and sheer determination.

Either way, the goal is the same: make something better than what we started with.

## Card: The Visitor's Guides
**Seven destinations. One very well-traveled developer.**

What started as regional print publications became a collection of WordPress websites helping visitors discover where to stay, eat, shop, and explore.

I migrated the sites from Drupal, developed their business directories and advertising features, and guided them through multiple hosting migrations.

Because a vacation planning website really shouldn't need a vacation of its own.

## Card: Coastal Virginia Magazine
**Twenty years of content. A whole new chapter.**

Migrating more than two decades of publishing content from TownNews to WordPress was just the beginning.

I helped transform the magazine's digital presence into a flexible publishing platform supporting monthly issues, advertising, analytics, and multiple related brands.

And because websites are never really finished, I continue improving accessibility, performance, and the systems keeping everything running.

## Card: Putt-Putt Fun Center
**One network. Two dozen locations. Zero need for two dozen headaches.**

Building a website for a franchise network sounds straightforward until every location needs its own hours, pricing, events, menus, and personality.

I developed a WordPress multisite platform that keeps corporate branding consistent while giving individual locations control over their own content.

In 2026, I audited and improved all 24 active franchise sites and the corporate site, bringing the network to an average health score of 95 out of 100.

## Card: Beach Events
**Less searching. More celebrating.**

Virginia Beach has no shortage of things to do. Finding them online shouldn't be the hard part.

I helped on the original Beach Events website, rebuilt it in 2021, and redesigned it again in 2023 with mobile visitors in mind, building an interactive calendar that makes it easy to discover events, explore details, and find tickets.

Because nobody should need a treasure map to find a concert.

## Card: Virginia Beach Hotel Association
**Giving the keys back to the people who need them.**

The association needed a website its own team could manage without calling a developer every time something changed.

I rebuilt their site with WordPress, adding member directories, event registration, job listings, and an editing experience designed for actual humans.

The result? More independence for the team and fewer unnecessary support requests.
