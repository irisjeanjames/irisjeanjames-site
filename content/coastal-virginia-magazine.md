## Twenty years of stories. A whole new chapter.

A magazine website has to do more than look good. It needs to publish on schedule, support advertising, help readers find stories, and give the editorial team a system they can actually work with. Preferably without calling a developer every time an issue goes live.

## The short version
- **My role:** Migration, development, upgrades, and ongoing technical operations
- **Website:** [Coastal Virginia Magazine](https://coastalvirginiamag.com/)
- **Migration:** TownNews (now BLOX Digital) to WordPress
- **Built with:** WordPress, Elementor Pro, AdRotate Pro, GeoDirectory, and IssueM
- **Related brands:** [Wine Fest](https://coastalvirginiawinefest.com/), [Burger Battle](https://covaburgerbattle.com/), and [Idea House](https://covaideahouse.com/)
- **Sister publication:** CoVa Business

## Moving the archives without losing the plot

I helped migrate more than 20 years of publishing content from TownNews to WordPress. That's a lot of stories, images, and history to bring along for the ride. Later, I moved the site off its original prebuilt theme and onto a Hello Elementor child theme customized for Coastal Virginia, giving the publication a more flexible foundation.

## The issue isn't finished until it's published

Print deadlines have a way of making themselves known. I built the templates and digital issue bookcases for each monthly edition, then helped make sure those issues actually went live on schedule. The website had to work with the rhythm of a real magazine, not the other way around.

## Advertising pays the bills. Details matter.

I manage advertising placements through AdRotate Pro and support the site's SEO reporting, so the team can see how the publication performs in search. An ad in the wrong place isn't just a design issue. It's a business issue.

## One publication, a family of brands

Coastal Virginia Magazine connects with Wine Fest, Burger Battle, and Idea House. I brought the magazine's branding into the header of each related site so they feel like part of the same family, without pretending they're all the same project.

## The 2026 audit: a little less waiting, a lot more accessibility

In October 2026, I audited a site with more than 12,000 images and an initial **C+** grade. One particularly unhelpful discovery: page caching was turned off, so pages were being rebuilt for every visit, with about **4.9 seconds** before loading began. I enabled caching and tackled structural accessibility issues, including page headings, main-content landmarks, descriptive article links, and teal text that needed stronger contrast.

Then came the image library. I used AI to draft descriptions, reviewed the output, and applied alt text to **12,035 images** through the database. The AI portion cost **$0.83**. I also added a rule that can derive alt text from clearly named new uploads. Technology is wonderful when it handles the repetitive parts and leaves room for human judgment.
